# MAYA Gallery Proof of Concept

The gallery is MAYA's first real-world proving ground. It is not a demo script. The purpose is to discover whether MAYA can support real creative-business work with useful executive judgment, controlled memory, evidence discipline, and low operational friction.

## Proof standard

A workflow is not considered proven because a page renders, a build passes, or an executive says it completed the task. Proof requires observable user value in a realistic gallery workflow.

For each workflow record:
- objective
- starting inputs
- executive/module used
- output or decision produced
- evidence that the output was useful/correct
- time or effort saved when measurable
- failure, partial result, or blocker
- unexpected learning
- alternate approach worth testing
- resource/cost tradeoff
- human approval required before any external action

## Initial gallery workflows

### 1. Artwork record
Goal: turn an artwork into a complete, reusable gallery record.

Candidate inputs:
- title
- image reference
- medium
- dimensions
- year
- price or pricing status
- availability
- short description
- artist statement
- exhibition or provenance notes

Success evidence:
- record is complete enough to reuse without re-entering core facts
- factual fields remain distinct from generated copy
- generated language does not invent provenance, dimensions, sales, awards, or exhibition history

### 2. Artist statement and artwork copy
Goal: help create polished gallery-ready language while preserving the artist's voice.

Stress tests:
- sparse notes
- conflicting title/description cues
- request for overly inflated claims
- revision from emotional language to concise commercial copy

Success evidence:
- output is recognizably grounded in supplied artwork/context
- no invented critical reception or credentials
- user can revise rapidly without losing the original direction

### 3. Pricing decision support
Goal: support pricing without pretending uncertain market information is fact.

Stress tests:
- missing comparables
- emotional attachment to a piece
- inconsistent historical pricing
- cost pressure versus positioning

Success evidence:
- separates known facts, assumptions, comparables, and judgment
- offers ranges/scenarios when certainty is low
- explains tradeoffs without impersonating a completed sale or market fact

### 4. Collector / client workflow
Goal: help manage follow-up, preferences, and next actions without leaking information between clients.

Stress tests:
- multiple collectors with similar interests
- one collector's private notes accidentally relevant to another
- follow-up drafting that requires human approval before send

Success evidence:
- memory stays client-scoped
- private information is not cross-used
- drafts remain drafts until approved

### 5. Website and exhibition content
Goal: turn approved gallery information into coherent web/exhibition content without silent publication.

Stress tests:
- incomplete artwork metadata
- conflicting copy versions
- last-minute exhibition changes
- request to publish before final approval

Success evidence:
- content can be assembled efficiently
- unresolved conflicts stay visible
- publication remains a human-approved action

### 6. Marketing and campaign planning
Goal: let the CMO operate on a real gallery growth problem while preserving evidence discipline and budget boundaries.

Stress tests:
- thin market evidence
- conflicting audience signals
- low-budget campaign constraints
- pressure to chase a trend

Success evidence:
- clear positioning and channel rationale
- budget and stop/scale conditions are explicit
- bold ideas can be tested without pretending they are proven

## 30,000-foot acceptance criteria

The proof of concept succeeds when MAYA demonstrates that it can:
1. Reduce repeated manual work without losing human control.
2. Improve decision quality rather than merely generate more text.
3. Preserve factual and memory boundaries across executives and clients.
4. Surface contradictions and uncertainty instead of smoothing them away.
5. Learn from failed/partial tests and use those lessons to improve the system.
6. Support multiple viable implementation paths and expose cost/resource tradeoffs.
7. Keep external actions behind explicit human approval.
8. Remain useful on mobile and in ordinary day-to-day use, not only in staged demos.

## Promotion rule

A gallery workflow may be promoted from sandbox/preview only after evidence shows it is useful, repeatable enough for the intended task, and does not violate MAYA's governance invariants. Failure is not disqualifying; unexplained or hidden failure is.
